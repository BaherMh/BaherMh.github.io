01 — RESEARCH

Ideas into impact.

Training-free compression and data-efficient speech generation, grounded in geometry and optimization.

NeurIPS 2026Accepted · First author

GeoPair

Geometry-Preserving Cross-Layer Factorization for Training-Free Transformer Compression

Shared-dictionary factorization that preserves each layer’s calibration geometry, combining generalized Sylvester solvers, Edmonds’ Blossom matching, and structured sparse coding.

Research details

Evaluated models up to 32B parameters without fine-tuning, including Phi-4 14B and Qwen3 32B. The method pairs layers and shares dictionaries while accounting for their distinct calibration geometry.

>94%

baseline average accuracy retained at 40% compression

NeurIPS 2026Accepted · First author

ROCKET

Rapid Optimization via Calibration-guided Knapsack-Enhanced Truncation for Efficient Model Compression

Calibration-guided compression with multi-choice knapsack allocation, single-step sparse factorization, and a closed-form dictionary update.

Research details

Retained over 90% of baseline performance at 30% compression without fine-tuning. Compressed Qwen3 14B to approximately 8B parameters; lightweight healing brought performance close to the native Qwen3 8B baseline.

>90%

baseline performance retained at 30% compression

NeurIPS 2026Accepted · Co-author

COMPOT

Calibration-Optimized Matrix Procrustes Orthogonalization for Transformers Compression

Training-free compression using orthogonal dictionaries, closed-form Procrustes updates, analytical sparse coding, and one-shot allocation of layer-wise compression budgets.

Research details

The framework combines calibration-aware matrix approximation with structured allocation and is compatible with post-training quantization.

One-shot

compression budget allocation, compatible with quantization

EMNLP 2026Accepted · Co-author

SEAM

Spectral Embedding Alignment and Merging for Low-Resource TTS

Zero-audio initialization for TTS vocabulary expansion, using spectral low-pass filtering and closed-form ridge regression to preserve phonetic order.

Research details

Reduced sample complexity by up to 75%, approaching or matching baselines using four times more data across three languages and two architectures, with significant human-preference gains.
